Abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models demonstrate promising generalization in robotic manipulation, driven by advances in large-scale vision and language pre-training. This progress can be misleading: despite strong zero-shot perception and language capabilities, task performance often degrades under distribution shifts, revealing gaps in how these systems translate high-level understanding into robust behavior.

We introduce Colosseum V2, a large-scale simulation benchmark for evaluating VLA generalization across diverse conditions. The benchmark comprises 28 tasks spanning 13 task categories and two robot morphologies, covering a wide range of manipulation primitives and long-horizon behaviors. Built on ManiSkill, Colosseum V2 enables fast, GPU-parallelized evaluation and supports both in-domain and out-of-domain testing at scale.

We evaluate state-of-the-art methods, including Action Chunking Transformers (ACT), π0.5, and MolmoAct2, and reveal limitations in both base performance and generalization. Hardware experiments show that simulation tracks relative robustness on real hardware, supporting the benchmark's ecological validity.

Benchmark Design

Colosseum V2 evaluates generalization along visual, language, and action axes. It uses two Franka Panda setups: a single-arm manipulator with a parallel-jaw gripper and a bimanual configuration with two Panda arms operating in a shared workspace. Tasks are organized from simple manipulation primitives through temporally extended, long-horizon behaviors. Note: MO stands for the Manipulation Object, the object that is directly manipulated, and RO stands for the Receiving Object, the object that is indirectly interacted with. These videos are from a third-person viewing camera that the models do not see. The models ingest three or four camera images that are not shown here.

Morphologies

  • Single-arm PJG. Sixteen tasks cover picking, pick-and-place, reorientation, insertion, tool use, articulated object interaction, and long-horizon manipulation: RaiseCube, PickSodaFromCabinet, PickDishFromRack, StackCube, PlaceBookInShelf, PlaceDishInRack, LiftPegUpright, RotateArrow, PegInsertionSide, PlugCharger, HammerNail, ScoopBanana, OpenDrawer, OpenCabinet, PlaceCubeInDrawer, and CookItemInPan.
  • Bimanual PJG. Twelve tasks test handovers, cooperative lifting, reorientation, insertion, articulated object interaction, and long-horizon dual-arm coordination: DualArmCubeHandover, DualArmBottleHandover, DualArmLiftPot, DualArmLiftTray, DualArmPushBox, DualArmPourPot, DualArmThreading, DualArmPenCap, DualArmDrawerPlace, DualArmDrawerOpen, DualArmStackCube, and DualArmStackTwoCubes.

Perturbation Axes

  • Visual. MO-Color, RO-Color, MO-Texture, RO-Texture, Table-Color, Light-Color, Table-Texture, Camera-Pose, Distractor-Objects, Background-Texture, and Background-Color.
  • Language. Language-Paraphrase, Language-None, Language-Other-Task, and Language-Random.
  • Action. MO-Size, RO-Size, and Pose-Randomization.
  • Combined. All perturbations applied at once.

Evaluation Protocol

Three language-conditioned imitation learning models are evaluated: ACT, π0.5, and MolmoAct2. A separate multitask policy is trained for each model and robot morphology. All three models are trained on 100 perturbation-free demonstrations per task and evaluated on every perturbation over 200 episodes. Raw MolmoAct2 results are in the appendix.

GPU-parallelized simulation makes the evaluation statistically thorough without making it prohibitively slow. On a single RTX 4090, ACT can be evaluated on both test suites in 11.5 hours while producing ten times as many success-rate measurements as the original Colosseum evaluation setup.

  • ACT: Modified version of ManiSkill's default ACT model. Adds a CLIP language embedding of the task description to the transformer's input. Source: jstmn/ColosseumV2 - act_clip/.
  • π0.5: Default π0.5 model from lerobot version v0.4.4. Trained with a batch size of 4 on an H100 until convergence. Note that due to breaking changes between lerobot version 0.4.4 and 0.6.0, you cannot run the π0.5 model we finetuned on the current lerobot repo. However, instructions for finetuning and evaluation of the model benchmarked in the paper are available at jstmn/lerobot_colosseum_v2@lerobot_0.4.3.
  • MolmoAct2: Default MolmoAct2 model from lerobot v0.6.1. Finetuned for 10,000 steps with a batch size of 32 on an A100. Only the action expert was finetuned due to GPU VRAM constraints (--policy.train_action_expert_only=true). Instructions for finetuning and evaluation this model are available at jstmn/lerobot_colosseum_v2.

Results and Analysis

Episodes from every policy, task, and perturbation can be browsed in the viewer below. Generalization and base success rates are shown below.

Generalization

To reduce noise, the generalization statistics below include only tasks whose no-perturbation success rate is at least 10% for the given model and morphology. The included task set therefore differs between models; per-task results are in the appendix.

Visual Generalization

  • ACT: Larger average success-rate drops under visual perturbations: 28.57% and 29.06% on the single-arm and bimanual suites.
  • π0.5: Smaller drops of 7.73% and 9.86%. We hypothesize that π0.5 benefits from larger pretrained visual representations.
  • MolmoAct2: Like π0.5, MolmoAct2 generalizes well to visual perturbations.

Language Generalization

Language is tested with four conditions: paraphrase, no instruction, an instruction from another task, and random English words.

  • ACT: Barely changes under paraphrase (−0.28% single-arm, −0.57% bimanual), which suggests that it largely ignores language conditioning.
  • π0.5: Substantially degrades task performance as the instruction leaves the training distribution: −63.6%, −72.2%, −87.8%, and −91.2% (bimanual) for paraphrase, none, other-task, and random. The ordering here is informative. Each successive perturbation is semantically farther from the training distribution, and the success rate declines accordingly.
  • MolmoAct2: Nearly negligible change in task performance, suggesting that, like ACT, it largely ignores the language component.

Action Generalization

  • ACT: The average relative decrease across the action perturbations is 11.2% (single-arm) and 18.24% (bimanual). Pose-Randomization is the primary contributor, at 21.8% and 40.9%. The bimanual drop is larger. We hypothesize this comes from greater spatial reasoning demands in the larger action space, and from joint-space control rather than the 6-DOF Cartesian control used on the single-arm suite.
  • π0.5: The average relative decrease is 4.11% (single-arm) and 10.08% (bimanual). Under Pose-Randomization, the decreases are 12.9% and 51.4%, again larger for bimanual tasks. MO-Size is neutral or positive: a larger manipulated object often makes the task easier, as in ScoopBanana.
  • MolmoAct2: The mean relative change across the action perturbations is +5.6% (single-arm) and −23.3% (bimanual). Pose-Randomization drops success by 29.8% and 27.4%. MO-Size is nearly neutral on the single-arm suite (−3.5%) but causes a 31.5% drop on bimanual. RO-Size raises success by +50% on the one included single-arm task (ScoopBanana) and −11.1% on bimanual. Note that the positive single-arm average is driven entirely by RO-Size on ScoopBanana; the other two action perturbations both reduce success. Only two bimanual tasks (DualArmPourPot and DualArmLiftPot) meet the 10% threshold.

Across morphologies, perturbations have remarkably consistent effects. The mean absolute difference in per-perturbation success rate change between single-arm and bimanual suites is 10.83% for ACT and 3.69% for π0.5, indicating that perturbation factors expose model-specific generalization behavior rather than only task-specific artifacts.

Base Success Rates

  • ACT: With no perturbations, mean success is 29.2% and 44.5% on the single-arm and bimanual suites. This is higher than both π0.5 and MolmoAct2: 1.37× and 3.53× versus π0.5, and 1.11× and 7.30× versus MolmoAct2. One possible explanation is a specialist versus generalist tradeoff: the two VLA models may be more generally capable and more robust to environmental perturbations, but they perform worse on these tasks.
  • π0.5: Mean success is 21.2% and 12.6% on the single-arm and bimanual suites.
  • MolmoAct2: Mean success with no perturbations is 26.4% on the 12 reported single-arm tasks and 6.1% on the seven bimanual tasks with a no-perturbation score.

Hardware Validation

To test whether simulation captures real-world robustness, five single-arm parallel-jaw tasks are reproduced on hardware: RaiseCube, RotateArrow, LiftPegUpright, PickDishFromRack, and PickSodaFromCabinet. A single multitask ACT policy is trained from scratch with 60 demonstrations per task. Each task is evaluated under None, MO-Size, MO-Color, Background-Color, Light-Color, Distractor-Objects, Language-None, and Table-Color, with 20 trials per task-condition pair.

Results

Real-world experiments indicate that Colosseum V2 does not precisely predict the absolute success rate on hardware for a particular task. In aggregate however, simulation tracks hardware closely: across all (sim, real) success pairs, the best-fit relationship is SRsim = 0.86 × SRreal + 15.1. This trendline is relatively close to SRsim = SRreal, illustrating that the overall trend holds despite per-task noise.

The relative effects of perturbations, measured against the no-perturbation baseline, are well preserved between simulation and hardware. After normalizing each perturbation by the corresponding no-perturbation success rate, the mean absolute difference between simulation and hardware is 12.3 percentage points. Colosseum V2 therefore estimates the relative robustness of a manipulation policy under distribution shift even when absolute success differs.
